## When should you call the fire department before anything else?

Call 911 immediately if there is active fire, visible embers in walls or attic space, or any smell of gas. Fire department clearance always comes before any restoration work, even a small, contained kitchen fire that looks like it's out. Firefighters check for hidden hot spots behind cabinets and in wall cavities that can reignite hours later.

Once the fire department releases the home, call your insurance company to open a claim, then call a smoke damage restoration company the same day. Soot is acidic and starts etching into painted surfaces, chrome fixtures, and electronics within hours, so the gap between "fire's out" and "restoration starts" matters more than most homeowners expect. For wildfire smoke infiltration with no fire on the property, call a smoke damage restoration company directly. If a home smells like smoke for more than a day or two after outdoor air quality improves, that's residue in the duct system and soft surfaces, not just lingering outdoor air, and it's a job for duct cleaning and [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) rather than an open window.

## What happens when you call a smoke damage restoration company?

The first call is a short phone triage, then a same-day assessment with a moisture meter, soot swab test, and air quality check to classify the damage before any cleaning starts. Have the fire department's clearance paperwork (if there was an active fire), your insurance policy number, and a rough timeline of when the smoke exposure started ready for that first call.

On arrival, a [Home Pride Restoration](/) technician walks the property room by room, testing soot type (dry smoke from a fast, hot fire behaves differently than wet smoke from a smoldering fire) and checking the HVAC system, since ducts recirculate soot particles through the whole house if they're not sealed off early. From there the crew sets up HEPA air scrubbers, seals off unaffected rooms, and starts [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) work: cleaning hard surfaces, treating porous materials that can be saved, and running thermal fogging or ozone treatment for odor once surfaces are clean. Work that involves structural fire damage, not just smoke, is scoped separately under [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/).

## What should you do while you wait for someone to arrive?

Don't run the HVAC system until it's been checked, since that recirculates soot through every room. Don't wipe soot with a wet cloth or household cleaner either; dry soot smears when it gets wet and becomes much harder to remove from drywall and upholstery, which is covered in more detail in [How to Clean Soot Off Walls After a Fire](/blog/how-to-clean-soot-off-walls/).

Open windows briefly for ventilation if outdoor air quality is good, photograph every affected room before anyone touches anything for the insurance claim, and separate smoke-damaged clothing and soft goods into bags rather than leaving them in closets, where the odor transfers to everything else nearby.
