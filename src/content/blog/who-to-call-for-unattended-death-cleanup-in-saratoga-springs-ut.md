**TL;DR:** If someone has died unattended in a home in Saratoga Springs, Utah, call 911 first so officers can respond and secure the scene. The Utah Office of the Medical Examiner determines cause of death and releases the property, usually within a few hours to a day. Only after the scene is released should a biohazard and trauma cleanup company be called to decontaminate the space. Insurance or the landlord is typically notified last, once cleanup is underway.

If you just discovered a family member, tenant, or neighbor who passed away days or weeks ago, you are likely still in shock and trying to figure out what to do in what order. This is not a situation anyone should try to manage alone, and it is not a cleaning job for you or a maintenance crew to attempt. There is a specific sequence of calls that protects your safety, respects the legal process, and gets the property back to a livable condition without making anything worse.

## Who do I call first when I find an unattended death?

Call 911 immediately. Law enforcement, usually the Utah County Sheriff's Office or Saratoga Springs Police, needs to respond before anyone else enters the space.

Do not move the person, touch surfaces, open windows, or try to clean anything, even if the smell is overwhelming. Officers will secure the area as a precaution until the medical examiner determines there is no suspicion of foul play. If you are inside the home when you find the person, step outside and wait for first responders rather than wandering through other rooms.

## Who releases the scene, the police or the medical examiner?

The Utah Office of the Medical Examiner determines the cause and manner of death and is the authority that releases the scene, not local police. Officers secure the property and may process it briefly for their own report, but the medical examiner's office has final say on when a cleanup crew can legally enter.

In most unattended-death cases without signs of a crime, release happens within a matter of hours. If the death is being treated as suspicious, the scene can stay closed longer while investigators finish their work. Either way, nothing involving decontamination can start until that release happens, so resist the urge to call a cleaning company before this step is done.

## When is it safe to call a cleanup company, and who should that be?

Once officers and the medical examiner have released the property, call a company trained specifically in biohazard and trauma scene cleanup, not a general house cleaner or a handyman. ## What should the family avoid touching or cleaning?

Do not touch, move, or attempt to clean anything in the affected room, including carpet, bedding, furniture, or personal items, until a trained crew has assessed it. Decomposition can soak into flooring and subfloor long before it's visible on the surface, and household cleaners will not neutralize the biohazard risk.

Avoid opening windows for long stretches to "air it out" before the crew arrives; this can push odor and contamination into HVAC ducts and other rooms. It's also wise to keep pets and children out of the affected area entirely. If you need to retrieve something urgent, like medication or documents, from elsewhere in the home, that's generally fine, but leave the room where the death occurred untouched.

## Does insurance cover unattended death cleanup, and who else should I notify?

Coverage varies a lot by policy and by whether the death is tied to a covered peril, so there is no universal yes or no answer. Some homeowners and renters policies include a trauma or biohazard cleanup endorsement; others treat it as an exclusion unless it follows a covered event. Review your policy language or ask your agent directly, and see the [Insurance Information Institute's overview of what standard homeowners policies cover](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) for general guidance.

If the home is a rental, the landlord needs to be notified once cleanup is scheduled, both for access and because many leases require it. Call the insurer or landlord after the cleanup crew is already lined up, not before; holding off on remediation to wait on a coverage decision only gives contamination and odor more time to spread. A reputable cleanup company can also document the work with photos and a written scope that supports a claim either way.

## What does odor removal involve after an unattended death?

Odor from an unattended death is not something an air freshener, ozone can, or a few days of open windows will resolve. Decomposition odor settles into porous materials like drywall, subfloor, and insulation, and it often requires removing contaminated materials entirely rather than just masking the smell.

After contaminated materials are removed and the area is sanitized, crews typically run hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging equipment to neutralize odor molecules in remaining porous surfaces, sometimes over several days.
