## When Do I Call a Restoration Company vs a Plumber, the Utility Company, or 911?

Call 911 or your utility provider first if there's a downed power line, a gas odor, or a structure that looks like it could collapse. No restoration crew should walk into an active electrical or gas hazard, and neither should you.

If the damage is wind, hail, or water intrusion through the roof, siding, or a broken window, with no immediate life-safety hazard, a restoration company is the right first call. That covers torn-off shingles, a tree limb through a section of roof decking, water pooling under a window after driving rain, or hail damage that's let moisture start tracking into an attic.

A plumber is the right call only when the problem is isolated to a supply line or fixture failure unrelated to the storm itself, like a burst pipe that happened to fail the same week. If you're not sure which category you're in, describe what you're seeing when you call. A restoration company that handles both [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) and plumbing-adjacent failures can usually tell you over the phone whether it's their job or a trade referral.

## What Happens When You Call?

When you call, expect to be asked what's actively happening: is water still coming in, is there a visible opening in the roof or a wall, and is anyone able to safely access the area. Have your address, a rough sense of which rooms are affected, and your insurance carrier's name ready, since that speeds up the first visit.

The first priority on arrival is almost always [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/): covering an open roof section or a broken window with plywood or heavy tarp material so wind and rain stop making the damage worse. After that, if water has already gotten into the structure, extraction and drying equipment goes in. If the roof itself needs a closer look, a [roof leak inspection and repair](/services/roof-leak-repair/) typically follows once the immediate exposure is controlled.

This two-step order, stabilize first, assess second, matters for your insurance claim too. Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and a tarp that goes up the same day documents that you did.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurer after the immediate hazard is stabilized, not before. Mitigation shouldn't wait on a claims adjuster, and most carriers expect you to act to limit damage regardless of when the claim gets filed.

Before you call, photograph the damage from multiple angles, note the date and approximate time of the storm, and keep any invoice or work order from the tarping or extraction work.
