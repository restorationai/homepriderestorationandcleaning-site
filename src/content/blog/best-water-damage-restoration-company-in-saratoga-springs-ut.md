## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Company in Saratoga Springs

The right water damage restoration company has three things: IICRC certification, 24/7 availability, and the ability to document the job for your insurance carrier. Everything else is secondary.

**IICRC certification** means the crew is trained to the S500 standard. That standard defines how to classify the water (Category 1 clean water, Category 2 gray water, Category 3 black water), how to measure moisture in walls and subfloors, how to set equipment, and how to confirm drying is complete. Without that training, a crew can pull up wet carpet, run a few fans, and declare the job done while moisture is still trapped in wall cavities. That hidden moisture becomes mold within days.

**24/7 availability** matters because water does not wait. Every hour of standing water increases the damage category and the repair cost. A company that opens at 8 a.m. is not the right call when your water heater fails at midnight.

**Insurance documentation** is where many homeowners get burned. A restoration company that hands you a vague invoice and no moisture logs leaves you exposed when the adjuster pushes back. Look for a company that provides a written scope of work before starting, daily moisture readings during the dry-out, and a final clearance report. That paper trail is what closes your claim cleanly. For more on what insurance typically covers, see our guide to [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job?

A professional water damage restoration job follows a defined sequence. Understanding the steps helps you ask the right questions and know whether the company you hire is doing the job correctly.

1. **Emergency contact and dispatch.** You call, they ask about the source of water, the affected area, and whether the water source is controlled. A 24/7 company dispatches immediately.
2. **Inspection and damage assessment.** The crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map the extent of the damage - not just what is visibly wet, but what is wet inside walls, under flooring, and in ceiling cavities.
3. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water. This step is time-critical: the faster the water is out, the lower the damage class.
4. **Drying and dehumidification.** Industrial air movers and LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers are placed based on the moisture map. The crew monitors readings daily and adjusts equipment placement as materials dry.
5. **Monitoring and documentation.** Daily moisture logs track progress. This documentation goes to your insurance carrier as proof of a complete, professional dry-out.
6. **Antimicrobial treatment.** If Category 2 or Category 3 water was involved, or if drying took more than 48 hours, antimicrobial treatment is applied to prevent mold growth.
7. **Final clearance.** Moisture readings confirm the structure is dry to pre-loss levels. A written clearance report closes the restoration phase.

If mold is found during the job or develops afterward, a separate [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) scope is written. For more on the timeline, see [how long does water damage restoration actually take](/blog/how-long-does-water-damage-restoration-take/).
