Smoke damage restoration is a different trade than carpet cleaning or general janitorial work, and the gap shows up in exactly what equipment and training walks through your door.

## What separates a real smoke damage restoration company from a general cleaning service?

A real smoke damage restoration company treats soot and smoke odor as a chemistry problem, not a surface-cleaning problem. Soot particles are microscopic and acidic, and they embed into drywall pores, insulation fibers, and HVAC ductwork, not just sit on top of surfaces the way dust does.

There are also different soot types that require different treatment. Protein soot, from a grease or kitchen fire, is nearly invisible but produces a strong, persistent odor. Synthetic soot, from burning plastics or furniture, is heavier and oilier and smears if wiped with the wrong agent. A carpet cleaning company's truck-mount extractor and general-purpose deodorizer were never built for either problem. They address the smell you can detect today, not the smoke residue sitting inside your wall cavities.

The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org/), the industry body that trains and certifies restoration technicians, publishes the standard that defines this work: the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/). A company working to that standard documents the type of soot present, selects cleaning agents accordingly, and verifies odor removal rather than just masking it.

## What certifications should a smoke damage company actually hold?

Ask specifically for IICRC Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician (FSRT) certification, not just a general "certified cleaning company" claim. FSRT training covers soot chemistry, structural cleaning methods, and odor-control equipment that general carpet or janitorial certifications don't touch.

A company's marketing can say "IICRC certified" without naming which individuals hold which credential or what standard they're trained to. Ask for the technician's name and registration number, the same way you'd ask a contractor for a license number. A legitimate company will have this on hand immediately, because the credential belongs to a specific person, not just the business name on the truck.

## What equipment should a smoke restoration crew bring to the job?

The equipment tells you more than the sales pitch does. A properly equipped smoke restoration crew typically brings a thermal fogger (which replicates the heat of combustion to drive deodorizing agents into the same pores smoke penetrated), hydroxyl generators or an ozone generator for unoccupied spaces, and HEPA air scrubbers to capture airborne soot particles during cleanup.

For contents, that is furniture, clothing, electronics, and keepsakes affected by smoke, a real restoration company documents and photographs everything before it leaves the property, moves it to a climate-controlled facility for ultrasonic or specialty cleaning, and returns an itemized inventory. If a company shows up with only a shop vac and an air freshener, that is the carpet-cleaning toolkit, not the fire-restoration toolkit.

## What questions should you ask before you hire a smoke damage company in Saratoga Springs?

A short list of direct questions separates a qualified crew from a general handyman service fast:

- Which technician holds IICRC FSRT certification, and what is their registration number?
- Do you handle both the structure (walls, ceilings, HVAC) and the contents (furniture, textiles, electronics) in-house, or do you subcontract one of those?
- Will you bill my insurance company directly, or do I pay out of pocket and submit for reimbursement?
- What equipment will you actually use for odor, thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment, or a scented masking spray?
- Will I receive a written scope of work before cleaning begins?

A company that answers all five without hedging has done this work before. One that gets vague on the equipment question is worth a second call.
