## What makes a sewage cleanup company the right choice in Saratoga Springs?

The right company is IICRC certified for Category 3 water, staffed to respond around the clock, and experienced billing insurance directly for sewage losses. Those four things separate a real restoration contractor from a general cleaning crew with a wet-vac.

Sewage backup is never a job for a handyman or a carpet cleaner with no containment protocol. Under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), any sewer or drain backup is classified as Category 3, also called black water: grossly contaminated water that can carry bacteria, viruses, and parasites. That classification drives everything that follows, from what gets removed to what PPE the crew wears.

## Is the company IICRC certified to handle Category 3 water?

Ask directly: is your technician IICRC certified, and do they follow the S500 Category 3 protocol? If the answer is vague, or they can't name the standard, keep calling.

Category 3 protocol isn't optional paperwork. It dictates that porous materials touched by sewage, carpet pad, baseboard, drywall below the flood line, often get removed rather than dried in place, because they can't be fully decontaminated. It also requires antimicrobial treatment of affected surfaces and a containment barrier to keep contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms.

Many Saratoga Springs homes built in the last fifteen years have finished basements sitting below the home's main sewer line. When a backup happens, that basement is exactly where Category 3 water pools first, often soaking into framing and insulation before anyone notices. A crew trained to the [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org/) standard knows to check behind baseboards and under subfloor, not just mop the visible puddle.

## Will they bill my insurance directly, and what's actually covered?

Most standard homeowners policies do not automatically cover sewer or drain backup. That coverage usually requires a separate water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement added to the policy, so check your declarations page before assuming you're covered.

This is one of the most common surprises homeowners hit after a backup: a burst supply pipe is typically a covered sudden loss, but sewage backing up through a floor drain is treated differently unless that endorsement exists. A good restoration company documents the loss thoroughly, photographs, moisture readings, a written scope, so you have what you need to find out from your carrier whether the endorsement applies, and to support the claim if it does. Home Pride Restoration and Cleaning handles that documentation and can work directly with your adjuster.

## What should you ask before hiring a sewage cleanup company?

Ask these five questions before anyone starts work in your home:

1. Are your technicians IICRC certified, and do you follow the S500 Category 3 protocol specifically?
2. What PPE does your crew wear for a sewage loss, and how do you contain the affected area from the rest of the house?
3. Are your technicians IICRC certified, and can I see proof of insurance?
4. Do you document the loss for insurance, and have you billed carriers directly before?
5. What happens to contaminated materials you remove, how are they disposed of?

A company confident in its process will answer all five without hesitation. Hesitation on any one of them is worth noting.
