Eagle Mountain is still one of the fastest-growing cities along the Wasatch Front, which means our crews spend more time on true final cleans, pulling protective film off new cabinetry, scraping texture overspray off tile, and clearing the fine gypsum dust that settles into HVAC returns during framing and drywall phases.

## Why Eagle Mountain Properties See Heavier Post-Construction Cleanup

Eagle Mountain sits in a high desert pocket of Utah County, and that matters for a final clean. Wind off the surrounding benches carries loose dirt and construction grit onto job sites even after the drywall and paint crews have packed up, so a "final" clean done elsewhere often needs a second pass here within days. New subdivisions going up across the city also mean back-to-back phases on the same street, concrete trucks, lumber delivery, roofing crews, all kicking up dust that drifts onto freshly cleaned windows and sealed floors next door. We build that into scheduling rather than treating the first pass as the last one.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a walk-through to flag anything a general cleaning crew would miss: paint overspray on hardware, caulk smears on glass, drywall mud dust packed into vent covers. From there the order matters. Ceilings and light fixtures get wiped and vacuumed first so dust falls onto floors we haven't touched yet. Windows, tracks, and sills come next, since construction film and labels leave residue that needs a scraper, not just glass cleaner. Floors get a two-stage treatment, dry vacuum to pull grit before it scratches new finishes, then a wet clean matched to the material (sealed concrete, LVP, tile, or hardwood all take different chemistry). Cabinets, counters, and fixtures are detailed last, and we do a final dust check along baseboards and in closets, areas a walk-through often skips but a home inspector or buyer won't.

## Local Note

One thing crews learn fast working in Eagle Mountain: gypsum dust here doesn't just sit on surfaces, it rides on the wind that comes down off the benches most afternoons. A final clean finished at 10 a.m. can have a visible film on south-facing windows by 3 p.m. if a neighboring lot is still in the framing or drywall phase. We schedule window and floor work as close to the final walkthrough as the builder's timeline allows, and when we can't control the timing, we tell the homeowner or project manager up front rather than letting them discover it at closing.
