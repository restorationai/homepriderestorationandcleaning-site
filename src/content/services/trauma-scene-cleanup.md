## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleaning covers the decontamination and restoration of spaces affected by death, violent injury, or unattended medical events, including blood, bodily fluids, and related biological material. The work differs from routine cleaning because it requires controlled containment of the affected area, EPA-registered disinfectants matched to the type of contaminant, and removal of any porous material (carpet, padding, sometimes subflooring or drywall) that can't be fully decontaminated. Our technicians work in full PPE, follow IICRC-informed protocols for bloodborne pathogen exposure, and treat every scene as a biohazard situation regardless of how it initially appears. Depending on the scope, a single-room job can take a few hours; scenes involving longer discovery periods or larger affected areas take longer because saturation runs deeper into building materials.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and containment.** We identify the affected area, set up containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and confirm safe access with any coordinating parties (family, property manager, law enforcement if still on scene).
2. **PPE staging and biohazard removal.** Technicians suit up in protective equipment rated for bloodborne pathogen exposure, then remove visible biological material and any non-salvageable porous materials following regulated handling procedures.
3. **Enzymatic and antimicrobial treatment.** Affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade enzymatic cleaners and disinfectants that break down residual biological matter at the material level, not just the visible surface.
4. **Odor neutralization.** Biological decomposition odors penetrate materials differently than smoke or mildew, so we use targeted deodorization methods suited to the specific contaminant rather than masking agents.
5. **Regulated waste disposal and documentation.** All biohazard waste is packaged and disposed of per state and federal handling requirements, and we provide the paperwork trail your insurance adjuster will ask for.

## What separates a good trauma scene cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake in this type of cleanup is treating it like a standard deep clean: surface disinfecting without addressing material saturation underneath carpet, subfloor, or baseboards. Biological material follows gravity and capillary action the same way water does, so a scene that looks resolved on top can still carry contamination below. A thorough operator tests beyond the visible stain boundary, removes porous materials that can't be fully sanitized rather than trying to save them, and documents every step with photos and a written scope, which matters both for insurance claims and for any legal or estate processes that may follow. Adjusters typically look for proof of proper biohazard waste manifests and a clear chain of custody for disposed materials, not just a description of the cleaning performed.
