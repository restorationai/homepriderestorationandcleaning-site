## What Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning actually involves

Fine drywall dust finds its way into places you wouldn't expect: inside HVAC returns, under cabinet toe-kicks, in the threads of door hinges, settled on top of light fixtures. A general cleaning crew with mops and all-purpose spray will make a renovated space look presentable, but it won't pull the silica dust out of carpet fibers or clear the haze off new windows without streaking. Post-construction cleaning is a separate trade from routine cleaning, and it's usually done in two or three passes rather than one.

The rough pass happens while the job site is still technically a job site: bagging debris, pulling protective floor film, knocking down heavy dust from walls and ceilings, and clearing adhesive and paint overspray from windows and fixtures. The detail pass is where the work actually earns the "final clean" label: cabinet interiors wiped out, baseboards and window tracks cleared of dust, light fixtures and vents cleaned, floors vacuumed and then wet-cleaned appropriate to the material (hardwood, tile, LVP, and carpet all take different chemistry). On larger remodels or new builds, a touch-up pass a few days before move-in catches the dust that resettles after HVAC systems run and finish trades do their last walk-through.

For projects that involved plaster, tile-setting, or spray finishes, the cleaning also has to account for what the dust actually is. Silica dust from drywall sanding and tile cutting is a respiratory irritant and gets handled with HEPA-filtered vacuums rather than swept or blown, which just redistributes it into the air.

## Our process

1. **Site walk and scope.** We walk the space with the builder, remodeler, or homeowner to identify what's finished versus still in progress, flag any surfaces needing special handling (natural stone, unfinished wood, new paint), and confirm what trades are still scheduled to come through.
2. **Debris and protective material removal.** Construction debris, packaging, leftover materials, and floor protection (Ram Board, plastic sheeting, blue tape) are cleared first so the actual cleaning isn't fighting through job-site clutter.
3. **HEPA dust removal.** Walls, ceilings, trim, vents, and light fixtures are vacuumed with HEPA filtration before any wet cleaning starts, because wiping dust before it's vacuumed just turns it into paste.
4. **Detail cleaning by surface type.** Cabinet interiors and exteriors, countertops, window glass and tracks, baseboards, door hardware, and plumbing fixtures are each cleaned with a method matched to the material, not a single all-purpose spray across everything.
5. **Floor finishing pass.** Floors are vacuumed, then cleaned according to material, hardwood and LVP get a damp microfiber pass, tile gets grout-safe cleaner, carpet gets a full extraction if it was exposed to dust during the build.

## What separates a good post-construction cleaning response from a bad one

The most common miss is cleaning order: wiping surfaces before dust is vacuumed off them, or cleaning floors before ceiling and wall dust has been knocked down, which means the floor just catches a second round of falling dust. Another frequent problem is using standard glass cleaner on new windows that still have construction film, adhesive, or paint speckle, it smears instead of lifting the residue, and the window ends up needing a razor-scrape pass anyway.

Builders and property managers doing a final walk-through generally check cabinet interiors, window tracks, and HVAC vent covers first, because those are the spots a rushed cleaning crew skips. A thorough job also protects finished surfaces still curing, like new paint or sealed grout, by using cleaning agents that won't react with them.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Utah's dry climate means drywall dust travels further and settles finer than it would in a humid region, since there's less moisture in the air to weigh it down. Builds that run through the winter months also tend to collect more tracked-in debris from snow and salt, which adds an extra layer to the floor-cleaning pass. Spring and summer, when most Saratoga Springs remodels and new builds wrap up, are the busiest season for final cleans, so scheduling the clean a week or two ahead of a target move-in date helps avoid a last-minute scramble.
