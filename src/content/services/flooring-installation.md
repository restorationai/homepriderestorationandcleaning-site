## What Flooring Installation actually involves

Flooring installation covers hardwood, laminate, tile, and vinyl plank, each with its own subfloor requirements, moisture tolerances, and installation method. Hardwood needs acclimation time and a subfloor within a specific moisture range. Laminate floats over an underlayment and needs room to expand at the edges. Tile requires a flat, rigid substrate or it will crack at the grout lines within a year or two. Vinyl plank is the most forgiving of the group but still needs a clean, level surface to lock together correctly.

Timeline depends on scope. A single room typically runs one to two days once old flooring is removed and the subfloor is ready. A full main floor with transitions, trim, and multiple material types usually takes closer to a week. Jobs that follow a water loss take longer because the subfloor has to test dry before anything new goes down.

## Our process

1. **Subfloor assessment.** We check for moisture, soft spots, unevenness, and squeaks before recommending a material. This step matters most when the old flooring is coming out because of a prior leak.
2. **Material selection and measurement.** We take an accurate takeoff of the space, account for waste and pattern matching, and confirm the product fits the subfloor type you have, concrete, plywood, or OSB.
3. **Removal and subfloor prep.** Old flooring comes out, nails and staples get pulled, and the subfloor is patched, leveled, or replaced where damaged.
4. **Installation.** Flooring goes down per manufacturer specification, with proper expansion gaps, underlayment, and transitions between rooms.
5. **Final walkthrough.** We check seams, transitions, and trim, and walk the space with you before calling it finished.

## What separates a good flooring installation from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the moisture check on the subfloor, especially in a home where flooring is being replaced after a leak or flood. Installing new material over a subfloor that reads dry on the surface but is still damp underneath leads to cupping, mold growth, or adhesive failure within months. Hardwood that goes in without proper acclimation time will contract or expand once it reaches room humidity, leaving visible gaps by the following season.

Another place corners get cut: expansion gaps. Laminate and engineered hardwood both need a gap at the walls to allow for seasonal movement. Skip it, and the floor buckles the first time humidity shifts. Vinyl plank installed directly over uneven concrete without a leveling compound will telegraph every dip and crack in the slab within a year.

When flooring installation follows a covered water damage claim, adjusters typically want documentation that ties the new flooring to the original loss: photos of the damaged material, moisture readings, and a clear scope showing what was removed and why. We provide that paperwork as part of the job, not as an afterthought.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Utah's dry climate along the Wasatch Front means indoor humidity swings more than homeowners expect, especially once furnaces run all winter. Hardwood and engineered wood need to acclimate to that dryness before installation, or you'll see gapping between boards by the first cold snap. Basements and crawl spaces in newer Saratoga Springs construction can also hold moisture longer than they appear to on the surface, which is why we test before installing anything that doesn't tolerate dampness well.
